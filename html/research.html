Research

Research

My work sits between statistics, machine learning and biology. It currently centers on two areas — single-cell and spatial multi-omics — with a growing interest in AI methods that make genomic analyses more reliable and reproducible.

01 Single-cell multi-omics

Modern single-cell assays measure several layers of biology in the same cell — gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and beyond. Each layer tells part of the story, and the interesting question is how to read them together into one coherent picture of what a cell is doing.

I develop and apply computational methods that integrate these layers, so that gene-regulatory programs can be studied as a whole rather than one modality at a time, and so that rare but important cell states are not lost in the noise.

scRNA-seqMultiome / ATACData integrationGene regulation

02 Spatial multi-omics

Spatial multi-omics measures biology while keeping the position of each cell in the tissue — not only gene expression but also chromatin accessibility, histone marks and other regulatory layers, each resolved in place.

I work on making these measurements usable in practice: handling the sparsity and noise that come with spatial data, identifying spatial domains that are stable across samples and platforms, and linking spatial regulatory signals — such as enhancer activity — back to gene expression and tissue organization.

03 AI & statistics for genomics

I am increasingly interested in applying modern machine learning — including large language models — to genomic problems in a way that is dependable rather than black-box.

That means benchmarking methods honestly, quantifying uncertainty, and building open, reproducible tools that other labs can actually use.

BenchmarkingUncertaintyReproducibilityOpen source

Where I am headed: I want to bring these threads together — methods that combine single-cell and spatial measurements with uncertainty-aware machine learning, so that a model’s confidence can be trusted alongside its predictions.

Context

Where I work

I am a graduate student in the Jialiang Huang Lab at the School of Life Sciences, Xiamen University. The group develops single-cell and genomic technologies and applies them to questions in gene regulation, development and disease. Within that environment I sit on the computational side, building and benchmarking the analyses that turn raw single-cell and spatial measurements into biological insight.
